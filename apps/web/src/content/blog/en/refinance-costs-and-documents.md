Refinancing has closing costs, like a purchase, although there’s no down payment and no deed taxes. Knowing them lets you calculate your break-even point properly.

## Typical costs

- **Lender fees:** origination, processing and underwriting, plus points if you choose to buy down the rate.
- **Appraisal**, except in some streamline refinances.
- **Title:** search and a new lender’s title policy.
- **Florida taxes on the new mortgage:** note doc stamps ($0.35 per $100) and the intangible tax (0.2%) may apply. How much you pay depends on whether there’s new money and how the loan is structured, so confirm it on your Loan Estimate.
- **Prepaids and escrow:** interest to your first payment and reserves for taxes and insurance.

Some lenders offer “no-cost” refinances, where the fees are covered by a slightly higher rate. Compare both options.

## Documents you’ll be asked for

- Recent pay stubs and W-2s or tax returns.
- Bank statements.
- Your current mortgage statement.
- Your current homeowners insurance policy.
- If you’re self-employed or use an ITIN, check the [document checklist](/en/tools/document-checklist/).

## The process

1. You review options and receive your Loan Estimate.
2. You lock your rate.
3. Appraisal and underwriting.
4. Closing. When refinancing your primary residence, federal law gives you three business days to cancel after signing (right of rescission).
