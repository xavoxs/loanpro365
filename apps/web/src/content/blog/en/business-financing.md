Not all commercial financing is for buying a building. Many companies need capital to operate and grow. These are the most common options.

## Equipment loans

They finance machinery, vehicles or technology. The equipment itself usually serves as collateral, which makes approval easier. Keep the loan term within the equipment’s useful life.

## Inventory financing and flooring lines

They let you buy merchandise before you sell it. Flooring lines are common for dealerships and businesses with high-value products: you pay off each unit when it sells.

## Accounts receivable loans

If your customers pay you at 30, 60 or 90 days, you can get liquidity using those invoices as collateral instead of waiting to collect.

## Unsecured lines of credit

A capital reserve you draw on when you need it, paying interest only on what you use. They usually depend on the business’s history and the owners’ credit.

## How to choose

- **One-time purchase** (equipment): term loan.
- **Gap between paying and getting paid**: receivables financing or a line of credit.
- **Seasonal inventory**: inventory financing.
