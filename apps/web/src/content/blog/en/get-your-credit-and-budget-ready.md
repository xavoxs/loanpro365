A lender decides with three things: your credit, your ability to repay and your savings. Preparing them before you apply can get you better terms.

## 1. Your credit score

Pull your free reports at **AnnualCreditReport.com** and dispute any errors. Then focus on what matters most:

- **Pay everything on time** from now on; a recent late payment weighs a lot.
- **Lower your card balances** below 30% of the limit.
- **Don’t open new credit** or finance a car while you prepare.

For reference, FHA allows 3.5% down from a 580 score and 10% down from 500. For conventional loans, Fannie Mae no longer sets a minimum score for files run through its automated system, although many lenders still ask for about 620. See how many points you need with the [credit plan](/en/tools/credit-plan/).

## 2. Your debt-to-income ratio (DTI)

It’s your monthly debt payments, including the future mortgage, divided by your gross monthly income. Many programs work around 43%, and some allow more with compensating factors. Paying down a card or a small loan can increase what you can buy. Try it in [how much can I afford?](/en/tools/how-much-can-i-afford/).

## 3. Your savings

You’ll need money for the down payment, closing costs and often reserves. Funds must be documented: avoid large unexplained deposits in the months before you apply. If a relative gives you money, it’s documented with a gift letter.

## 4. Your documents

Start gathering them now: pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, bank statements and an ID. If you’re self-employed or use an ITIN, the list changes: check the [document checklist](/en/tools/document-checklist/).

With your credit and budget clear, the next step is choosing the loan that fits you best.
