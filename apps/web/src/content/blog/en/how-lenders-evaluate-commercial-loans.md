In a commercial loan the property takes center stage: the lender wants to know whether it earns enough to pay the debt. These are the numbers it looks at.

## Net operating income (NOI)

What the property earns in a year: rents and other income, minus operating expenses such as taxes, insurance, maintenance, management and utilities. It does not include the mortgage payment.

## Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)

Divide NOI by the annual debt payment. A DSCR of 1.25 means the property earns 25% more than it costs to pay the loan. Each lender sets its own minimum by property type; below 1.0 the income doesn’t cover the debt. Try it in the [DSCR calculator](/en/tools/dscr-calculator/).

## Loan-to-value (LTV)

How much they lend against the appraised value. Commercial loans usually require more equity than home loans; the percentage depends on the property type and the lender.

## You and your experience

Lenders also review your credit, your reserves and your experience running similar properties, especially for hotels, construction or repositioning.

## Typical documents

- Current rent roll and leases.
- Property operating statements for recent years and year to date.
- Personal and business tax returns.
- Personal financial statement and proof of funds.
- Purchase contract, if it’s an acquisition.

Preparing them early speeds up underwriting and improves your options. The last step: financing your business operations.
