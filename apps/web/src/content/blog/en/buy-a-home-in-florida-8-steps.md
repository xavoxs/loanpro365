Buying in Florida takes preparation, especially in a competitive market. This guide walks through the whole path and where buyers usually slip.

## 1. Review your finances and set a budget

Add up income, savings and debts. Beyond the down payment, set money aside for closing costs and taxes. Check your credit score: if it is low, waiting a few months to improve it can pay off. Use the [payment calculator](/en/tools/payment-calculator/) to see your real monthly payment with taxes, insurance and HOA.

## 2. Get pre-approved

A pre-approval letter states how much you may borrow and shows sellers you are serious. You will need proof of income, bank statements and tax returns. As brokers, we compare several lenders to find the best rate. Start with our [4-step pre-qualification](/en/prequalify/).

## 3. Choose the area

Consider schools, safety, commute, services and transportation, and the type of home: single-family, condo or townhouse. For condos, review the association fees.

## 4. Find a trusted real estate agent

A good agent filters listings, joins you on showings and negotiates for you. Look for local experience, references and, if you prefer, Spanish.

## 5. Search for your home

List your must-haves: bedrooms, yard, garage, neighborhood. Listing sites help you explore, but always visit in person.

## 6. Make an offer and negotiate

Your agent helps set a price based on recent sales. The offer includes price, closing date and contingencies such as inspection or final loan approval. Once accepted, you usually put down an earnest money deposit that counts toward your down payment.

## 7. Inspect the home

Hire a licensed inspector to check structure, roof, electrical, plumbing, air conditioning and appliances. In Florida, look closely for termites, moisture and hurricane damage. With the report you can ask for repairs, renegotiate the price or walk away.

## 8. Close and get your keys

At closing you sign the mortgage and the deed and pay the closing costs and the rest of the down payment. Estimate your [Florida closing costs](/en/tools/florida-closing-costs/) beforehand.

**After closing:** activate homeowners insurance (and flood insurance if needed), change the locks and store your documents somewhere safe.

## Mistakes to avoid

- An unrealistic budget that ignores insurance, HOA and maintenance.
- House hunting without a pre-approval.
- Going without an agent or a mortgage advisor.
- Deciding on emotion instead of numbers.
