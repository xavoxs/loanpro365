Refinancing means replacing your current mortgage with a new one. It can save you money, but it also has costs. The question isn’t only “did rates drop?” but “how long until I recover what it costs?”.

## Good reasons to refinance

- **Lower your rate or monthly payment.**
- **Switch from an adjustable to a fixed rate** for a stable payment.
- **Shorten your term**, for example from 30 to 15 years, to pay less interest overall.
- **Take cash out** of your home equity to consolidate expensive debt or renovate.
- **Remove mortgage insurance** once you have enough equity.

## The break-even point

Divide the refinance closing costs by what you save each month. The result is the number of months it takes to recover the cost. If you plan to stay in the home longer than that, refinancing likely makes sense.

Example: if refinancing costs $6,000 and you save $200 a month, you recover the cost in 30 months.

Run your own case with the [refinance calculator](/en/tools/refinance-break-even/): enter your current payment, the new amount, the new rate and the costs, and see your savings and how many months to recover them.

## Signs it isn’t the right time yet

- You plan to sell or move before reaching the break-even point.
- The monthly savings are small compared with the closing costs.
- Restarting a 30-year term would make you pay more interest overall, even if the payment drops.
- Your credit has dropped since you got your current mortgage.

If the numbers work, the next step is choosing the type of refinance that fits you.
