Choosing the right loan can change your down payment, your monthly payment and what you pay in mortgage insurance. These are the four most common options.

## FHA

Insured by the Federal Housing Administration. Designed for buyers with smaller savings or credit that is still being built.

- **Down payment:** from 3.5% with a 580 score; 10% with scores from 500 to 579.
- **Mortgage insurance:** an upfront premium of 1.75% of the loan and an annual premium (0.55% for most 30-year loans).
- **Best for:** first-time buyers or people rebuilding credit.

## VA

Guaranteed by the Department of Veterans Affairs for veterans, active-duty service members and some eligible spouses.

- **Down payment:** often 0%.
- **Monthly mortgage insurance:** none; there may be a funding fee.
- **Credit:** VA sets no minimum; each lender sets its own.

## USDA

Backed by the Department of Agriculture for eligible rural and some suburban areas, with income limits.

- **Down payment:** can be 0%.
- **Best for:** moderate-income buyers outside large urban centers.

## Conventional

Not government-insured; follows Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules.

- **Down payment:** from 3% for eligible first-time buyers (for example, HomeReady), 5% otherwise.
- **Mortgage insurance (PMI):** only below 20% down, and it can be removed as you build equity.
- **Credit:** since November 2025 Fannie Mae sets no minimum in its automated system; many lenders ask for about 620.

## Which one is yours?

It depends on your score, your savings, your military history and where you want to buy.
