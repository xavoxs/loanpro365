Before thinking about mortgages, answer one question: should you buy now or keep renting? There is no single answer. It depends on how long you plan to stay, what you pay today and what owning would cost.

## What you pay when you rent

Rent is simple: one monthly payment that usually rises every year. In return you can move easily and you don’t pay for major repairs. What you don’t build is equity: that money doesn’t come back to you.

## What you pay when you buy

Owning costs more than the mortgage payment. In Florida, also add:

- **Property tax**, which the Homestead Exemption can reduce if it’s your primary residence.
- **Homeowners and wind insurance**, plus flood insurance in some areas.
- **HOA or condo fees**, if any.
- **Maintenance** and repairs.
- **Closing costs** when you buy and **selling costs** when you sell.

In return, every payment lowers what you owe and the home can appreciate. That is equity.

## The break-even point

Buying usually pays off if you stay long enough for the equity you build to outweigh the cost of getting in and out. If you plan to move within a year or two, renting is almost always cheaper. If you plan to stay many years, buying usually wins.

Run your own case with the [rent vs. buy calculator](/en/tools/rent-vs-buy/): enter your rent, the home price and how long you plan to stay, and see the year buying starts costing less.

## Signs you may be ready

- Your income is steady and you plan to stay in the area for several years.
- You have savings for a down payment and an emergency cushion.
- Your monthly debts are under control.

If that sounds like you, the next step is getting your credit and budget ready.
